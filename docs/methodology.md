# Methods and interpretation

amr-clonalshare reports several quantities with different targets. A comparison must preserve phenotype meaning, lineage resolution, retained isolates, units and the target of uncertainty. A confidence interval is not a sampling-representativeness test.

## The lineage share of a call

`attribution.clonal_share` evaluates lineage means on held-out isolates. Isolates are assigned to folds within lineages: the lineages are taken in random order and their isolates assigned to the folds in turn, so that a lineage of m isolates spreads over min(m, k) folds and every held-out isolate of a repeated lineage keeps a member of its lineage in training. A held-out isolate predicted by the mean of t training members of its lineage has an expected squared error of its lineage's variance times 1 + 1/t; the score removes the cost 1/t lineage by lineage, from the unbiased within-lineage variance, and the baseline, the training grand mean, pays its own cost in the same way. Both corrections are exact in expectation whatever the variance of each lineage. The score (`kappa`), averaged over `repeats` fold assignments, is set against the mean score of `n_perm` permuted labellings (`null_mean`), each scored on one fold assignment: `kappa_adj = (kappa - null_mean) / (1 - null_mean)`, zero where the labels predict as permuted labels do and one at a perfect prediction. The estimate is not clipped and can fall below zero near the permutation null. It is computed on the isolates of repeated lineages, with singletons set aside and counted (`n_singletons_set_aside`) and untyped isolates and missing values dropped and counted; its support, the share of typed isolates in repeated lineages, is reported with it, and it is estimable with at least two repeated lineages. It describes prediction for further isolates of the represented lineages, not for lineages that were not observed.

Its interval (`observed_low`, `observed_high`) is for the lineage share of the represented lineages: the lineages and their sizes held fixed and the isolates of every lineage drawn again from its distribution, the between-lineage share of the variance. It is a studentized (bootstrap-t) smoothed bootstrap. The isolates of every lineage are drawn again from the smoothed distribution of its readings, the Dirichlet posterior mean under Perks' prior of total mass one over the K categories of the strata its isolates were read in, (count + 1/K) / (m + 1). A category is a reading within a stratum, so the stratum of every isolate is drawn with its reading, and a lineage whose readings all fall in one category is drawn with a small chance of every other category of its strata. Every draw is scored as the data were; the share is computed as its average over the fold assignments, set against the permuted control of the data, and given a normal draw with the spread that averaging a finite number of fold assignments leaves in the estimate. Every draw is studentized, t = (share − η\*) / se, with η\* the share of the smoothed distribution itself, computed exactly, and se the root of the sum of three variances: the infinitesimal jackknife of the fold-averaged share, with the variance of each lineage taken under its smoothed distribution so that a lineage whose isolates share one value is not given a variance of zero; the spread left by the fold assignments; and the variance of the share where the lineages do not differ, read from the permuted labellings, without which the interval would collapse to a point where the lineages read alike. The labellings permuted for the control are studentized in the same way, t = share / se, each with the share averaged over the fold assignments, the same fold spread and its own standard error, and the quantiles of the interval are taken no closer to the estimate than theirs: q<sub>0.975</sub> is the larger of the two 97.5% points and q<sub>0.025</sub> the smaller of the two 2.5% points. Under exchangeable labels this is the distribution of the data's own studentized share, and a studentized permutation test keeps its level asymptotically where the lineages share only their mean (Janssen, 1997; Chung and Romano, 2013), so an interval that leaves out zero is one that test rejects; and where the lineages hold few isolates, pairs above all, the bootstrap scenario, whose lineages draw from their own few readings, understates how far the share can be misread. The interval is (kappa_adj − se q<sub>0.975</sub>, kappa_adj − se q<sub>0.025</sub>), with se the standard error of the data (`observed_se`).

Its upper end is then raised where one lineage alone can account for more of the share. The distribution of one lineage ranges over the empirical-likelihood region of its own readings, the distributions on the categories it was read in with 2 m<sub>g</sub> KL(P̂<sub>g</sub> ‖ P) at most the 95% point of F(1, m<sub>g</sub> − 1), while every other lineage keeps its readings; the upper end is at least the estimate plus the largest rise of the share that any such distribution, for any lineage, gives. This answers for a share accounted for by a few lineages of few isolates, one of which happens to read close to the rest, where the standard error at the readings is too small. Both ends are reported within 0 and 1, so an interval wholly below zero is [0, 0]. The interval is reported when at least 90% of the `n_boot` draws give a share; otherwise the record says how many did.

The permutation p-value compares the between-lineage sum of squares with its values under the permuted labellings, (b + 1) / (n_perm + 1) after Phipson and Smyth, so it cannot fall below `p_floor = 1 / (n_perm + 1)`. The labels are exchanged within strata where strata are given and, where a sampling-unit column is declared (`unit_column`), within units as well, on a set of permutations of its own: a test of lineage differences within units, exact when the isolates of one unit are exchangeable, whatever the units share. For a call and no strata it is the permutation form of the chi-square test of the lineage-by-outcome table. The cross-validated score is not the test statistic. The estimate, its control and its interval do not change when units are declared; they describe the represented lineages and units. Across antimicrobials the p-values enter the Benjamini-Yekutieli step-up at `evidence.alpha` (`lineage_selection`), which is valid whatever the dependence between agents. The smallest attainable p-value has to fall below the first threshold of the step-up for a single agent to be selectable; the default 999 permutations clear it for a panel of up to 15 agents at 0.05, and a larger panel needs more.

## MIC measurements

The MIC result every run reports is the lineage share of the MIC ordering (`order` in the record, `mic_order` in `results.csv`). Within each stratum, the testing laboratory named by `mic_panel_column` crossed with every declared covariate (`mic_covariate_columns`), the readings define the nonparametric maximum-likelihood estimate of the MIC distribution (Turnbull), computed by the constrained Newton method of Wang, and every reading (a, b] is scored by its mid-distribution position: the share of that distribution below a plus half the share between a and b. For exact readings, and for a stratum read on one panel, this is the mid-rank less one half, over the number of readings; for censored readings it is the Wilcoxon score of Peto and Peto and of Prentice. The share of these scores accounted for by lineage is estimated by the estimator of the call, with the lineage labels permuted only within strata; the p-value is the permutation form of the Kruskal-Wallis test on the scores within strata, exact under exchangeability within strata whatever the distribution of the readings. In the interval the categories are the distinct readings of every stratum, and the scores are recomputed from the readings of every bootstrap draw, so that the uncertainty of the scores, which depend on the distribution of the readings, is part of it.

The score is the mid-distribution transform on which the rank intraclass correlation of Tu, Li, Zeng and Shepherd (2023) is defined, so the share is the analogue of that correlation for the observed lineages, extended to censored readings on several panels. It is invariant to any increasing transformation of the MIC scale. A constant offset between strata drops out, and a different panel does not appear as a difference between lineages, though a coarser panel resolves less of the ordering; a difference between lineages read only in different strata drops out as well. With a single cut point the score is 1/2 + (call − prevalence)/2 within each stratum, so the share is that of the call centred on its prevalence within each stratum, and that of the binary call itself when there is one stratum. Readings on an end well are tied at the panel's edge, and a panel with many of them shows less of the lineage ordering than there is; `share_end_wells` is reported with the share. The p-values of the agents enter the Benjamini-Yekutieli step-up as those of the calls do (`order_selection`). The ordering share uses the budgets of the `attribution` section.

The readings are read on the log2 scale as intervals (`censored.py`): a reading at a tested dilution lies above the previous tested dilution and at or below this one, and a reading on the lowest or highest well is censored unless `censored.end_wells_censored` is false. Explicit operators take precedence over the end-well rule. Configured agent-specific wells (`mic_wells`) fix the panel; without them the tested wells are every doubling between the lowest and the highest recorded value when the readings sit on a doubling lattice, and the recorded values otherwise, inferred within each panel label. Units are retained without conversion. The MIC collection includes eligible MIC-only isolates. When each stratum is read on one panel, the ordering of the readings, and with it the share and the bounds below, does not depend on how an end well is read.

## The lineage share of the latent ordering

`latent_order.py` bounds the lineage share of the ordering of the latent MICs. Within a stratum s let F<sub>s</sub> be the distribution of the latent MIC and U = F<sub>s</sub>(MIC), which is uniform in every stratum. The share is rho = Var(E[U | lineage]) / Var(U) = 12 Σ<sub>g</sub> w<sub>g</sub> (E[U | g] − 1/2)², the rank intraclass correlation of the latent MIC within strata, with the represented lineages weighted by their share w<sub>g</sub> of the isolates. U is pooled over strata, so opposite lineage effects in two strata cancel; `latent_order_lower_within_strata` reports the lower bound of every stratum on its own beside the pooled one. A call bounds the same share for the continuous value it thresholds, as a reading with one cut point (`call_bounds`, reported beside the share of every call).

A reading (a, b] says only that U lies in [F<sub>s</sub>(a), F<sub>s</sub>(b)], and how the isolates of different lineages are ordered inside one reading is not observed, so rho is not identified. The readings identify, for every reading r, its range [L<sub>r</sub>, H<sub>r</sub>], its share P(r) of the isolates and the share π<sub>gr</sub> of it that lineage g holds. By Strassen's theorem on the convex order, the lineage means allowed inside reading r form the base polytope of the submodular function f<sub>r</sub>(A) = π<sub>r</sub>(A) H<sub>r</sub> − (H<sub>r</sub> − L<sub>r</sub>) π<sub>r</sub>(A)² / 2, and the lineage means over all readings form the base polytope of Σ<sub>r</sub> P(r) f<sub>r</sub>; as they range over it, rho ranges over an interval [rho<sub>min</sub>, rho<sub>max</sub>] (Theorem 1 of the module).

- `latent_order_lower`, rho<sub>min</sub>, the lower bound the readings establish, minimises a convex function over the polytope. It is computed by Wolfe's minimum-norm-point algorithm, whose linear step is Edmonds' greedy vertex, after lineages spread over the readings in the same proportions are merged, which leaves the minimum unchanged. It is reported less the larger of its certified gap (`latent_order_gap`) and 1e-10, and never below zero, so it never exceeds the minimum.
- rho<sub>max</sub> is attained at a vertex, one order of the lineages, the same in every reading. With at most 12 lineages it is found exactly by dynamic programming over the sets of lineages. With more, an insertion search from eleven orders finds a large share and a branch and bound over the orders, with at most 500 bounds computed, certifies an upper bound on rho<sub>max</sub>; with at most 20 lineages the dynamic programme is run wherever the two do not meet. `latent_order_upper` is the largest share attained, a share the readings allow, and `latent_order_upper_bound` the certified upper bound; `latent_order_upper_exact` says whether they agree. Every share between the lower end and `latent_order_upper` is allowed by the readings.
- `latent_order_sharp` is true when the distinct readings of every stratum are disjoint intervals, as they are when a stratum is read on one panel, and the bounds are then sharp. Where readings of one stratum overlap, every run of overlapping readings is treated as one cell within which the isolates may stand in any order: the set of arrangements considered contains every arrangement the readings allow, so the lower end remains a lower bound and the upper end an upper bound, and they need not be attained. The lower confidence limit counts only readings that lie wholly below one another and holds in both cases.

The bounds rest on two assumptions: each reading holds the MIC it stands for (for a call, the side of its cut-off), and the latent MIC is continuous within a stratum. No distributional form is assumed for the latent values. Given those, the bounds are a theorem, not a confidence statement: applied to the observed readings, they contain the lineage share of the ordering of the latent values of the observed isolates, in every dataset. On the same isolates and strata a finer panel never lowers the lower bound, and a call, one cut of a panel, never raises it; the bounds of a call contain those of any panel it was cut from.

The lower confidence limit (`latent_order_lower_limit`) is a statement about the population of the represented lineages. For every vector z of lineage scores, rho ≥ 12 G(z)₊² / Q(z) (Theorem 2 of the module), where G(z) sums, over strata and ordered pairs of lineages, the weighted differences of their scores times P(Y<sub>h</sub> < Y<sub>g</sub>) − 1/2, the probability that a reading of lineage h lies wholly below a reading of lineage g in the stratum less one half, and Q(z) is the isolate-weighted variance of the scores. This dual certificate needs only that every reading holds its MIC, neither one panel per stratum nor coarsening at random; with one panel per stratum its largest value over z is rho<sub>min</sub>. For a fixed z, G(z) has an unbiased estimate, a U-statistic over pairs of isolates. Within every lineage-by-stratum cell the isolates are split at random into two halves, and the split is used both ways: in each direction one half chooses z, the minimum-norm point of its own readings, and the other half estimates G(z) without bias, scaled by √Q(z). The limit averages the scaled estimates over both directions of 25 random splits (`latent_order_splits`): the average has an expectation below √(rho<sub>min</sub>/12), and its standard deviation is at most the average of their standard deviations whatever the dependence between the splits. With Student's t at the smallest of their Satterthwaite degrees of freedom, L = 12 max(0, mean − t<sub>0.95</sub> × mean sd)² lies below rho<sub>min</sub>, and so below rho, with probability at least 95%, to the accuracy of the normal approximation to the U-statistics. The variance of each estimate is that of its first-order projection, estimated from all isolates of every cell under the cell's distribution smoothed by Perks' prior over the readings of its stratum, plus a bound on the variance of the pair terms; pairs with a lineage absent from the estimating half of a stratum are counted at the worst case. The limit is reported at most at `latent_order_lower`, and `latent_order_alpha` (0.05) records its level. It treats the readings of different isolates as independent given the represented lineages (and any sampling units), conditions on the number of isolates of every lineage in every stratum, and sets singleton lineages aside.

`panel_resolution`, one less the expected squared width of a reading on the scale of positions, is the share of the ordering the panel resolves; `latent_order_midpoint`, the share when every isolate sits at the middle of its reading, is the resolution times the plug-in share of the scores.

## Two lineage definitions

`comparison.compare_lineage_definitions`, and `amr-clonalshare-compare` on the command line, compare two recorded lineage definitions of one binary outcome on an explicit frame of records. This is a descriptive sensitivity analysis, not a causal decomposition. Six analyses, the first definition on its available records, on the records common to both definitions and on the common scorable records, and the second definition on the same three frames in reverse order, separate a change of the included records from a change of labels. The common scorable records are those that sit in a repeated lineage under both definitions, found by removing isolates until every remaining one has a partner under each. The five differences between consecutive analyses sum exactly to the total difference: record selection into the shared frame, isolates set aside as singletons under the first definition, relabelling on the common scorable records, isolates set aside as singletons under the second definition, and record selection out of the shared frame. Only the middle term changes the labels on the same scored isolates, and the three middle terms sum to the change of definition on the common records; `identity_residual` records the identity.

The paired intervals (`paired_intervals`) are 95% bootstrap-t intervals for the five terms, the change on the common records and the total. The records and both labellings are held fixed, and the outcome of every record is drawn again from the smoothed distribution of its cell, the records that share both labels (Jeffreys' posterior mean rate, (positives + 1/2) / (records + 1)). The six analyses are recomputed on the same drawn outcomes, each set against its own permuted control, and every term is studentized by its own standard error, the infinitesimal jackknife of the shares over the records with the variance of every cell taken under its smoothed distribution, plus the fold spread of the distinct analyses (arms that score the same records under the same labels are one analysis), and centred on the terms of the drawn scenario, computed exactly with the distribution of every lineage that of a record drawn from it, so that a lineage whose records sit in cells of different rates carries that spread within it. An interval is therefore for the change the terms describe on these records and labels, with the outcomes as the random part. The intervals are computed only when every analysis is estimable, and reported when at least 90% of the draws give all six shares (`paired_n_boot_used`). Each analysis also carries its own interval for its represented lineages; two such intervals do not make an interval for their difference.

## Two collections and incomplete observation

The Kitagawa identity decomposes the prevalence of the first level of `contrast_levels` minus that of the second into composition and within-lineage terms. A lineage observed in one collection only is given, in the other, its observed rate, which places its whole contribution in composition, where it is reported apart (`nonshared`, with `composition_shared` the remainder). Shared support, the unweighted mean of the two collections' shares of isolates in lineages common to both, governs both components: below `surveillance.min_shared_support` (0.9 by default) both are marked not estimable. The limits are bootstrap percentiles, with isolates resampled within each collection, or whole sampling units where `unit_column` is declared. Benjamini–Yekutieli adjustment applies within each reported component family and does not repair selection. A supported calculation may describe the typed subset despite missing labels; collection generalization remains unsupported whenever labels are missing. Equal typing fractions and nonsignificant association checks do not establish representativeness.

Missing binary outcomes produce exact recorded-frame prevalence bounds from the extreme all-negative and all-positive completions. These are not confidence intervals or imputed estimates. The reported prevalence point concerns observed outcomes; the bounds concern all isolates in the stated recorded frame. Neither establishes a parameter outside that frame.

## Evidence

Fixed-look evidence and sequential evidence are distinct. The fixed-look e-value of an antimicrobial is a split likelihood ratio: lineage probabilities are fitted, with empirical-Bayes shrinkage, on the training folds and scored on the held-out isolates against the null probability maximised on those isolates, and the ratios are averaged over folds and repeats; e-BH across the panel controls the false discovery rate at one look under arbitrary dependence. With `batch_column`, the sequential construction fits on every earlier intake and scores the next, and the running product is a test supermartingale that may be read after any intake; the rule for the whole programme declares an agent once its running product reaches m/alpha. The sequential construction uses past-only prediction and requires the specified conditional null model and the actual batch order. It is not an unrestricted permission to reuse fixed-look tests, union rejection sets or search grouping definitions. E-values measure evidence, not the magnitude of a share.
